## Slide 4

FROM SESSION 2
You already know the equation
Net worth  =  what you own  −  what you owe
Move one term across:
Assets  =  Liabilities  +  Equity
Same sentence, rearranged. Not a rule somebody invented — it is arithmetic, and it cannot fail to balance.

[Notes] Do the rearrangement on the board. Students find the accounting equation mysterious; it is the net worth formula they computed for themselves in Session 2.\n\nSay explicitly: it balances because it is a definition, not because accountants are careful.

## Slide 5

25 MINUTES  ·  SIX TRANSACTIONS
Watch it breathe
A business opens. Six things happen. After every one, both columns move and the two totals stay equal.
# | What happens
1 | Owner puts ₹1,00,000 into the business
2 | Buys equipment for ₹40,000, cash
3 | Buys ₹30,000 of stock, on credit
4 | Sells half the stock (cost ₹15,000) for ₹25,000 cash
5 | Sells the other half for ₹25,000 — on credit
6 | Pays the supplier ₹30,000
Do not write anything yet. We build each one on the board together.

[Notes] Draw two columns on the board and work transaction by transaction. Ask what moves BEFORE writing anything.\n\nThe three that teach the most: #2 (an asset swap — nothing on the right moves), #3 (both sides grow together, and it is the bulk deal from Session 5), and #5 (profit appears in equity with no cash anywhere).

## Slide 7

TRANSACTION 2 OF 6
Buys equipment for ₹40,000, cash
ASSETS — what we own
Cash ₹60,000
Equipment ₹40,000
OWED + OWNERS' SHARE
Equity ₹1,00,000
Both sides: ₹1,00,000

[Notes] AN ASSET SWAP. Cash became equipment. The right-hand side did not move at all — ask them why before saying it. The business is no richer and no poorer.

## Slide 8

TRANSACTION 3 OF 6
Buys ₹30,000 of stock, on credit
ASSETS — what we own
Cash ₹60,000
Equip ₹40,000
Stock ₹30,000
OWED + OWNERS' SHARE
Payables ₹30,000
Equity ₹1,00,000
Both sides: ₹1,30,000

[Notes] BOTH SIDES GROW. You now own ₹30,000 more stock and owe ₹30,000 more, and nobody paid anything.\n\nTHIS IS THE BULK DEAL FROM SESSION 5. When the chai stall prepaid ₹1,800 of dry goods, cash fell and stock rose — an asset swap. Here the supplier gives credit instead, so both sides grow. Ask which version the stall would have preferred, and why.

[Notes] The first profit. Sold something costing ₹15,000 for ₹25,000, so ₹10,000 lands in equity. Cash went up by the full ₹25,000, and stock fell by ₹15,000.

## Slide 10

Cash ₹85,000

OWED + OWNERS' SHARE
Payables ₹30,000
Equity ₹1,20,000

[Notes] THE IMPORTANT ONE. Identical sale, identical ₹10,000 of profit into equity — and not one rupee of cash. The money is sitting in 'owed to us'.\n\nThis is the office next door, on a balance sheet.

You made ₹20,000 and you have ₹45,000 less cash. Where is it?
₹40,000 is standing in the corner as equipment. ₹25,000 is in somebody else's pocket. You already knew both — the balance sheet just tells you exactly which pocket.

## Slide 13

How a real one is arranged
ASSETS
Non-current — things that stay
Property · plant · equipment · long-term investments · goodwill

Current — things that turn over within a year
Stock · money owed to us · cash
LIABILITIES + EQUITY
Equity — the owners' share
What was put in · profits kept in the business

Non-current liabilities
Long-term borrowings

Current liabilities
Suppliers · short-term debt · tax due
Current assets minus current liabilities is working capital — the number you met at the stall, now with an address.

[Notes] Current means within twelve months. Everything liquid sits at the bottom of assets and the bottom of liabilities, which is where you look first.\n\nAsk: which side does a session pass sit on for the class store? Liability — money taken for something not yet delivered. They have been on both sides of that one.

## Slide 14

BOOK VALUE vs MARKET VALUE
Is it worth what the page says?
Book value
Assets minus liabilities, as recorded. Mostly what things cost, less what has been written off.
Market value
What people will pay for the whole company today. A number the balance sheet does not contain.
A software company's best asset walks out of the building every evening. It is on no balance sheet.
Which is why market value sits far above book value for brands and software, and close to it for steel plants and cement.

[Notes] Goodwill deserves one sentence: when a company buys another for more than book value, the difference sits on the balance sheet as goodwill. If the purchase turns out to have been a mistake it gets written down — a real expense for something that was never physical.

## Slide 15

GROSS DEBT vs NET DEBT
One number, two ways of saying it
Reliance Industries, as at 31 March 2020:
₹3.36 lakh cr
Gross debt — what it owed
₹1.75 lakh cr
Cash it was holding
₹1.61 lakh cr
Net debt — the difference
Both numbers are true. They tell you different things.
Net debt asks
If you used your cash to pay down debt, what would be left?
Gross debt asks
What do you actually owe, and to whom, and when?

[Notes] VERIFIED: gross debt ₹3.36 trillion, cash ₹1.75 trillion, net debt ₹1.61 trillion as at 31 March 2020.\n\nNet debt is the cleaner measure of financial strength; gross debt is what has to be refinanced. A company with huge cash and huge debt still has to roll the debt over, and the cash may be trapped in subsidiaries or other countries.

## Slide 17

THE CAVEAT
Now read it like an analyst
Of the ₹53,124 crore rights issue, only 25% — about ₹13,281 crore — was actually received in that financial year. The remaining 75% came the following year.
The full amount was counted in the announcement. Every rupee was legally committed, and shareholders had signed up — but it had not arrived.
Is the claim wrong?
No. Committed capital is real, and the company was open about the phasing.
Is it the whole picture?
Also no. 'Net debt free' on the day meant a promise of cash, not cash in the account.
Committed is not collected. You learned that at a chai stall — the office next door had committed too.

[Notes] VERIFIED, and the best part of the case. Do not present it as a gotcha — Reliance disclosed the phasing and the deals were genuine.\n\nThe lesson is about reading. A headline balance-sheet claim can be technically accurate and still require you to check when the money arrives. Same distinction as the stall, five orders of magnitude larger.\n\nWorth adding: the net-debt-free status did not last. The company took on debt again for expansion. A balance sheet is a photograph of one day, and somebody chose the day.
