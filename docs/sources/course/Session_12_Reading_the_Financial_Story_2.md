## Slide 4

30–38 MIN  ·  HOW THEY LOCK TOGETHER
Four sessions, three statements, one set of events
Statement | The question it answers | Period or point | Built in
Income statement | Did we earn it? | Over a period | S6–S7
Balance sheet | What do we have, and who has a claim on it? | At a point in time | S8
Cash flow statement | Did the cash actually move? | Over a period | S9
The three locks: profit flows into equity on the balance sheet · the change in the cash line on the balance sheet is the bottom of the cash flow statement · the cash flow statement starts with the profit the income statement reported.
You have verified all three on the six transactions. Today you do it on a company with ₹54,000 crore of revenue, and the arithmetic is no harder.

## Slide 5

## Slide 7

48–54 MIN  ·  TEST THEM
Four answers, and what each is worth
“Costs grew faster than revenue”
Check it. Operating profit nearly doubled, 637 to 1,208. Operating costs did not outrun revenue
“Depreciation and interest ate it”
Closer. Both roughly doubled. Together they took ₹1,989 cr against operating profit of ₹1,208 cr
“They took a big write-off”
Reasonable guess, and wrong here. Nothing in the ladder is missing
“Tax”
Partly true and worth noticing: the tax rate went from 24% to 40%. But that is an effect, not the cause
Nobody in the room will get it from the income statement, because the explanation is not in the income statement.

## Slide 8

54–58 MIN  ·  THE PART NOBODY READS
It was in the segment note
“During the quarter ended June 30, 2025, the Group started transitioning from its marketplace model to a combination of marketplace and inventory-led model in its quick commerce segment. Owing to this change, the revenue under quick commerce segment now includes amount on account of direct sales to customers on the Blinkit platform and not just the marketplace commission on such sales…”
Eternal FY26 annual report, segment note — page 275 of 409.
Before: a marketplace
Somebody else owned the biscuits. Eternal took a commission and reported the commission. A ₹500 order might appear as ₹60 of revenue.
After: first-party
Eternal owns the biscuits. The whole ₹500 is revenue and the cost is an expense. Same order, same rupees of margin.
Reported revenue cannot be read as growth until you adjust for the model change

## Slide 11

Revenue is a reporting choice
as much as a business outcome.
This business grew hard and fast — 126% like for like at Blinkit, which is a very good number. The reported 169% (61% like for like) is a different claim about a different thing, and the two are not comparable. Nothing was hidden and nobody broke a rule; the number simply stopped meaning what it meant last year.

## Slide 12

THE USE OF THREE
One statement barely flinched
 | FY25 | FY26 | What the change tells you
Revenue | 20,243 | 54,364 | Mostly the accounting model
Net profit | 527 | 366 | Real, and driven by depreciation, interest and tax
Cash from operations | 308 | 632 | Real, and it doubled
Reported profit fell and operating cash flow doubled, in the same year, at the same company. If you had read only the P&L you would have concluded the business got worse. If you had read only the cash flow statement you would have concluded it got better. Neither reading is complete, and that is the point of having three.
A model change can move revenue. It is much harder to move the bank balance.

## Slide 13

65–68 MIN  ·  THE HABIT, NOT THE RECIPE
Four kinds of knowing, and do not mix them up
What the figures establish
Revenue rose to ₹54,364 cr. Profit fell to ₹366 cr. Operating cash doubled.
Audited. You can cite it.
What a note explains
Blinkit moved to an inventory-led model from Q1 FY26; like-for-like growth was 126%.
Disclosed. Cite the note.
What is your inference
That the model change is also why the cash cycle flipped to +2 days.
Defensible. Say “this looks like”, not “this is because”.
What needs a question
Whether the thinner cycle is permanent or unwinds as scale builds.
Nobody has published it. This is the sixth line.
The segment note is not always the answer. The lesson is that statements, notes, inference and management each answer a different kind of question — and a good analyst never passes one off as another.

## Slide 14

68–78 MIN  ·  THE TEMPLATE
Five sentences, in this order, every time
1
What it sells, and to whom
No jargon. If you cannot say it plainly you have not understood it
2
Where the money comes from, and which part is growing
Income statement, revenue and segments
3
The one cost choice that explains the margin
Income statement, cost structure
4
What it owns, what it owes, and who has a claim
Balance sheet
5
Whether the profit became cash, and what was done with it
Cash flow statement
Five sentences. Not five paragraphs, and not a tour of the line items.

## Slide 15

WORKED, OUT LOUD
Eternal FY26, in five sentences
1
It delivers restaurant food and groceries to people's homes, and owns the stock in the grocery half.
2
Revenue tripled on paper because Blinkit started buying the goods; food delivery, which the change did not touch, grew 26% and still earns most of the profit.
3
Margins are thin by construction — 2.2% at the operating line — because delivery is a cost per order, not a cost that spreads.
4
It has no borrowings at all — the ₹4,592 cr a screener calls debt is lease liabilities on stores — against ₹30,980 cr of equity.
5
Profit fell to ₹366 cr, but operating cash doubled to ₹632 cr, and that cash went to repaying and investing rather than out of the door.
Every number in those five sentences came from a statement you can open. None of them came from a press release.

## Slide 16

NAME THE CHOICE, NOT THE MARGIN
Sentence 3 is the one that costs marks
Describing the margin is not naming the choice that causes it. Work along this chain, in this order.
BUSINESS MODEL
How it makes money
Eternal delivers each order to one address
COST STRUCTURE
What that forces it to spend
A rider cost per order — a cost that does not spread over volume
OPERATING MARGIN
What falls out
2.2% — thin by construction, not by mistake

## Slide 17

THE SIXTH
And then the line that is not a sentence
The one question the statements cannot answer, that you would put to management
Too weak
“What is your growth strategy?”

Could be asked of any company in any year, and the answer would not change anything you do.
Strong
“The cycle went from (54) days to +2 in one year. Is that the permanent cost of owning inventory, or does it come back as scale builds?”
The difference is not length or vocabulary. It is whether the answer would change what you do.

2 different business

Revenue | 54,364 | 20,290
Operating margin | 2.2% | 14%
Net profit | 366 | 1,547

Shape | +  +  − | +  −  −
Tata Consumer's revenue grew about 13% a year for four years and its operating margin never left the 13–15% band. Eternal's revenue has grown faster than any number in this course and its margin has never reached 4%.
Neither is the better business. They are being run for different things, and the statements say which.

## Slide 19

READING HABITS
What to notice, and what to ask about it
When you see | Do not conclude | Ask instead
Revenue jumping | The business grew | Did the definition change? Segments, policy notes
Cash well below profit | Something is wrong | Which working capital line, and is it growth or collection?
Cash well above profit | The business is strong | Is it depreciation, or is it suppliers being paid late?
A big 'other income' | The profit is fake | Is it recurring, material, and related to the core?
An adjusted number | They are hiding something | What exactly was adjusted out, and would you adjust it out?
Margins falling as revenue rises | They are losing control | Is this a mix shift, or is the core getting worse?
None of these is a verdict. Every one of them is the start of a conversation, which is what a financial story actually is.
