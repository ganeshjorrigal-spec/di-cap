About 70 seconds. Three numbers, both companies. First, cash against profit. Over three years Dabur's cash from operations was 121% of its profit, and above 100% in every single year. Marico's was 99% over two years, and only 82% in FY25, when working capital absorbed 293 crore. Second, the cash conversion cycle. Dabur's is minus 14 days: its suppliers are paid after its stock has been sold and collected, so they fund its working capital. Marico has to fund 34 days of it with its own money. Third, the cushion. Dabur has 8,426 crore of net cash; Marico 2,221 crore. Together these say how much of a bad year each business could absorb without asking anyone for money. One more sign: Dabur's gross margin held at about 48% while Marico's fell from 50.3% to 44.5% in a single year as copra costs rose. If asked: CCC is receivable days plus inventory days minus payable days, closing balances, with inventory and payables on cost of goods built from materials, purchases and change in inventories.
