# Part C: The argument

## 8. I back Dabur

**The deciding difference.** Dabur's profit turns into cash more reliably, and its suppliers, not its shareholders, fund its working capital. Marico earns more on its capital today, but its FY26 numbers lean on commodity prices and newly bought brands, and its cash has not kept pace with its profit every year.

| | Dabur | Marico | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash from operations ÷ group profit | 121% (FY24 to FY26) | 99% (FY25 to FY26) | Cash flow statements and P&L (calculated) |
| Cash conversion cycle, FY26 | −14 days | +34 days | Balance sheets and P&L (calculated) |
| Net cash (cash and investments − borrowings), FY26 | ₹8,426 cr | ₹2,221 cr | Balance sheets (calculated) |

Why these three: together they say how much of a bad year the business could absorb without new money. Dabur's cash has exceeded its profit in each of the last three years; Marico's fell to 82% of profit in FY25 when working capital took ₹293 cr. Dabur's negative cycle means it needs no money of its own to fund stock and customer credit; Marico must fund 34 days of it. And Dabur's gross margin held at about 48% while Marico's moved from 50.3% to 44.5% in one year.

**The conditions.** My backing holds only if:
1. Dabur's operating margin stops falling: at or above 15% in FY27 and FY28 (FY26: 15.0%).
2. Revenue growth rises above 6% a year (FY22 to FY26: 4.9%).
3. The ₹9,489 cr of cash and investments is put to work in the business or returned, not left to grow.

## 9. The strongest case against me

Marico is simply the better business. It earns a **41.9% ROE against Dabur's 16.6%**, and a **48.1% ROCE against 20.0%**. Its revenue has grown twice as fast (9.4% a year against 4.9%), with India volumes up 8% in FY26. Dabur's operating margin is 3.4 points lower than in FY22 although its gross margin held, so the slide is in costs Dabur controls. A quarter of Dabur's profit before tax is interest on cash it has not found a use for. A strong balance sheet that is never used is not a strength; at some point it is just a lower return.

**My answer.** Most of that is true, and it is why my backing has conditions. Two parts of it are weaker than they look:
- **The ROE gap is partly funding, not operations.** Marico's assets are 2.39 times its equity against Dabur's 1.53, and much of Marico's extra is non-bank liabilities, including ₹1,585 cr of other current financial liabilities that include obligations to buy out minority owners of acquired brands. On the operating business alone, with cash and investments removed, Marico still earns more (about 94% against 59%), so I accept it is the more efficient business today.
- **Resilience is the question I was asked.** Efficiency in a good year and survival in a bad one are different tests. Marico's FY26 shows how quickly a commodity can move its gross margin by six points; Dabur's three years show profit becoming cash with suppliers carrying the working capital.

So the point at which Dabur's caution becomes simple underperformance is the number below.

**What would change my mind.** If Dabur's operating margin is below 15% in FY27 while its revenue grows less than 6%, I would switch to Marico.
