## Option 1: I back Marico

**8. The deciding difference.** Marico earns far more on the capital its business actually uses. It turns every ₹1 of assets into ₹1.35 of sales; Dabur turns ₹1 into ₹0.75, because more than half of Dabur's balance sheet is cash and investments and it runs more of its own plants.

| | Dabur | Marico | Statement, FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ROCE | 20.0% | 48.1% | P&L and balance sheet (calculated) |
| Revenue growth, FY22 to FY26 per year | 4.9% | 9.4% | P&L (calculated; FY22 from Screener) |
| Where the margin fell | Below gross profit: gross margin 48.0% → 48.3%, operating margin 18.4% (FY22) → 15.0% | At gross profit: gross margin 50.3% → 44.5% as material costs rose to 55.5% of revenue | P&L; Marico investor presentation |

Why the third row matters: Dabur's margin is slipping in costs it controls (employees, overheads, depreciation), which looks structural. Marico's fell on an input price (copra) it passed on, which is cyclical, and its operating profit still grew 8%.

**Conditions.** (a) Marico's gross margin recovers towards its FY25 level as copra eases; (b) its buy-outs of acquired brands are paid from operating cash, not borrowing (borrowings stay near ₹355 cr); (c) cash from operations stays at or above profit.

**9. The strongest case against.** Dabur is the more resilient business. Its profit has become cash every year (121% over three years against Marico's 99% over two, with FY25 at 82%), suppliers fund its working capital (cycle −14 days against +34), and it has ₹8,426 cr of net cash against ₹2,221 cr. Marico depends on one commodity-linked product for 36% of India revenue, and it has taken on ₹1,585 cr of other current financial liabilities, including obligations to buy out minorities.

**My answer.** All of this is true and it is why my backing has conditions. But Dabur's extra safety is mostly a ₹9,489 cr investment book that earns treasury returns and pulls its ROE down every year it sits idle, and its operating margin is 3.4 points below FY22 while its gross margin held, so there is no commodity shock to blame. Marico's ₹2,576 cr of cash and investments cover its ₹1,585 cr of current other financial liabilities, and its FY26 cash conversion was 115%.

**What would change my mind.** If Marico's operating margin is not back above 17% by FY28, or its cash from operations falls below profit in FY27, I switch to Dabur.

## Option 2: I back Dabur

**8. The deciding difference.** Dabur's profit turns into cash more reliably, and its suppliers, not its shareholders, fund its working capital. Marico's FY26 numbers depend on commodity prices and newly bought brands.

| | Dabur | Marico | Statement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash from operations ÷ profit | 121% (FY24 to FY26) | 99% (FY25 to FY26) | Cash flow and P&L (calculated) |
| Cash conversion cycle, FY26 | −14 days | +34 days | Balance sheet and P&L (calculated) |
| Net cash, FY26 | ₹8,426 cr | ₹2,221 cr | Balance sheet (calculated) |

Supporting: gross margin held at 48% at Dabur while Marico's swung from 50.3% to 44.5% in one year.

**Conditions.** (a) The operating-margin slide stops: at or above 15% in FY27 and FY28; (b) revenue growth moves above 6%; (c) the treasury is either invested in growth or returned, not left to grow.

**9. The strongest case against.** Marico is simply the better business: ROE 41.9% against 16.6%, ROCE 48.1% against 20.0%, revenue growing twice as fast, and India volume growth of 8%. A quarter of Dabur's profit before tax is interest on idle cash, and its operating margin has fallen from 18.4% to 15.0% in four years.

**My answer.** Much of Marico's higher ROE is leverage from non-bank liabilities (assets ÷ equity 2.39 against 1.53) and Dabur's idle cash, not only better operations. I accept Marico earns more per rupee today. I back Dabur because the question is resilience, and Dabur's cash conversion and working capital would carry it through a bad year that Marico's commodity exposure might not.

**What would change my mind.** If Dabur's operating margin is below 15% in FY27 while revenue grows less than 6%, I switch to Marico.
