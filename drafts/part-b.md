# Part B: Marico

*Consolidated, ₹ crore, same definitions as Part A, closing balances.*

**What it sells, and to whom.** Marico sells coconut and value-added hair oils, Saffola edible oils and foods, and a growing set of premium and digital-first personal-care brands to Indian households through the trade and, increasingly, online. In FY26 Parachute coconut oil was 36% of India revenue, value-added hair oils 18% and Saffola edible oils 17%. International business (Bangladesh is about 45% of it) grew 20% in constant currency (Q4 FY26 investor presentation).

| | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 10,831 | 13,611 |
| Gross margin (built) | 50.3% | 44.5% |
| Operating margin | 18.1% | 15.6% |
| Net margin (owners' profit) | 15.0% | 12.9% |
| ROCE | 46.7% | 48.1% |
| ROE | 41.0% | 41.9% |

**DuPont, FY26:** ROE = (1,762 ÷ 13,611) × (13,611 ÷ 10,075) × (10,075 ÷ 4,210) = 0.1295 × 1.3510 × 2.3931 = **41.9%**, equal to 1,762 ÷ 4,210. FY25: 0.1504 × 1.2999 × 2.0961 = 41.0%. Margin fell, but turnover and leverage rose more.

**Why FY26 needs care.** Revenue grew 26%, but India value growth was 28% on only 8% volume: most of it is price passed on as copra costs rose. Material costs went from 49.7% to 55.5% of revenue. That is why the gross margin fell while operating profit still grew 8% (₹1,961 cr to ₹2,126 cr). FY26 also consolidates acquisitions (4700BC from 29 Jan 2026, Cosmix from 5 Feb 2026, True Elements taken to 100%), so it is not fully comparable with FY25.

**Cash conversion cycle (closing balances):**

| Days | FY25 | FY26 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Receivables (₹1,271 cr → ₹1,304 cr) | 42.83 | 34.97 | −7.86 |
| Inventory (₹1,235 cr → ₹1,611 cr) | 83.66 | 77.79 | −5.87 |
| Payables (₹1,306 cr → ₹1,634 cr) | 88.47 | 78.90 | −9.57 |
| **Cycle** | **38.02** | **33.86** | **−4.16** |

The three changes add to the change in the cycle (−7.86 − 5.87 + 9.57 = −4.16). Caveat: acquisitions add full closing balances but only two months of sales and costs, so FY26 days are distorted in a direction I cannot determine from the statements.

**Profit against cash.** FY25: profit ₹1,658 cr, cash from operations ₹1,363 cr, **gap −₹295 cr**, because working capital absorbed ₹293 cr (receivables up ₹205 cr, payables down ₹218 cr). FY26: profit ₹1,813 cr, cash ₹2,084 cr, **gap +₹271 cr**, helped by a ₹362 cr rise in other financial liabilities and ₹282 cr more owed to suppliers.

**Funding and risk (FY26).** Borrowings ₹355 cr against owners' equity ₹4,210 cr: **debt ÷ equity 0.08** (0.13 with leases). Interest cover **40.1 times**. Net cash ₹2,221 cr. But the DuPont leverage factor of 2.39 is high for a debt-free company because of other liabilities: other current financial liabilities rose from **₹288 cr to ₹1,585 cr**, which includes obligations to buy out minority shareholders in acquired brands. These are not bank debt, but they are real future cash calls.
